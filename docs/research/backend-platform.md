# Research track: backend-platform

## Summary
The 2026 backend-platform landscape shifted materially against managed options for a studio like this. Microsoft replaced PlayFab's Development Mode with "Foundation Mode" on March 11, 2026: the free tier dropped from 100,000 player accounts to 1,000 lifetime account creations unless the game ships on Xbox and is linked to Partner Center — PC-only/mobile-first titles have no comparable path, and Insights/Event Export were retired March 31, 2026. Community consensus reads this as PlayFab becoming an Xbox customer-acquisition channel rather than a neutral backend. Epic Online Services remains free and cross-platform but is thin exactly where Dragon Heroes is hard: Player Data Storage has write rate limits and no queryable server-side economy, so studios use EOS only for auth/voice/NAT/Easy Anti-Cheat and pair it with a custom backend for inventory and trades. AccelByte is solid but enterprise-priced (PCCU-metered, roughly $0.055–0.11 per peak-concurrent-user/day across modules, plus per-seat tooling) and is explicitly positioned for funded AAA-scale teams. Nakama (Heroic Labs) remains the strongest open-source anchor: Apache-2.0, v3.39.x current, ~12.8k stars, 103 releases, actively maintained, runs on PostgreSQL or CockroachDB, and natively covers accounts/auth (device, email, social), groups/guilds, chat, server-side matchmaker, leaderboards, and a built-in tournaments API; Heroic's paid Hiro GDK adds economy/inventory/achievements/store, and Satori 2.0 adds live-ops (feature flags, experiments, live events, personalization) — useful for weekly content drops, though these two are commercial, not open source.

For the never-dupe-never-lose real-money economy, the clear pattern from fintech practice is to NOT keep RMT state in a game backend's generic storage: use PostgreSQL with an append-only double-entry ledger (every trade debits one account and credits another; balances sum to zero; mistakes fixed by compensating entries, never updates), unique item-instance rows with single-owner constraints, an escrow state machine for trades (listed → escrowed → settled/refunded), idempotency keys on every mutating API call, and SERIALIZABLE isolation or explicit row locks with retry/backoff. Nakama's own wallet is server-authoritative with a transaction ledger and is fine for soft currency, but the marketplace deserves a small dedicated "economy core" service (Go or NestJS/TypeScript) that is the sole writer to the money/item tables, with reconciliation jobs asserting invariants (ledger sums to zero, no item owned twice). A single managed Postgres with PITR and a synchronous replica is sufficient and simpler than CockroachDB at small-studio scale; Cockroach only pays off if you later need multi-region writes (Nakama supports both since it speaks the Postgres wire protocol).

On the Brazil-specific money flows: do not touch funds directly. Marketplace-model PSPs with native Pix split payments — Mercado Pago (split for marketplaces, real-time Pix settlement), Pagar.me, Asaas (Pix capped ~0.99%, no monthly fee), plus Pix-specialists like Woovi/OpenPix and infrastructure providers like QI Tech/Celcoin — let the buyer pay into the PSP, hold funds until your server confirms item transfer, auto-split your fee, and credit the seller's sub-account with Pix withdrawal, while the PSP performs seller KYC/KYB and MED 2.0 fraud handling. This keeps you outside Banco Central "instituição de pagamento" licensing, which tightened in 2025–2026 (Resolução BCB 506/2025; mandatory authorization window May 2026 for pre-existing unlicensed players; R$15k per-transaction caps for unauthorized institutions). For tournament prize pools funded by marketplace fees: Brazilian tax law treats performance-based competition prizes as remuneration, and cash prizes from contests generally attract 30% IRRF withheld by the payer (DARF code 0916, remitted by the 3rd business day after the decêndio), with the Receita Federal actively building tooling around bets/virtual-competition prizes in 2026 — so payouts must collect CPF, withhold tax, and run through the PSP's KYC'd Pix payout rails; avoiding player entry fees keeps you clearly on the skill-competition side rather than under Lei 14.790/SPA betting licensing. Anti-cheat: full server authority kills state-fabrication (speed hacks, teleports, dupes, damage injection) by construction; what remains client-side to worry about is input automation/bots (valid inputs at superhuman rates), information exposure (only send clients state they should see — interest management is your wallhack defense in a 2D ARPG), timing manipulation (lag switches, disconnect-on-death), and account/marketplace fraud — mitigated by server-side plausibility/rate validation, statistical behavior detection, match replays for tournament review, economy anomaly monitoring, and optionally free Easy Anti-Cheat via EOS on PC builds.

## Recommendations
- Anchor the meta-game on self-hosted Nakama (Apache-2.0, on managed PostgreSQL): it covers accounts/auth, guilds (groups), chat, matchmaker, leaderboards, and has a built-in weekly-tournaments API — one system replaces most of what you'd buy from PlayFab/AccelByte; budget real DevOps time or fall back to Heroic Cloud if ops overwhelm the team.
- Build ONE small custom 'economy core' service (Go preferred for a single static binary; NestJS acceptable if the team is TypeScript-native) that is the exclusive owner of real-money items, marketplace, and prize-pool state — Nakama calls into it via RPC; never let game servers or Nakama storage write RMT tables directly.
- Model the economy as an append-only double-entry ledger in PostgreSQL: item instances with unique IDs and a single-owner constraint, escrow state machine for trades (listed → escrowed → settled/refunded), idempotency keys on all mutations, SERIALIZABLE isolation (or SELECT FOR UPDATE) with retry/backoff, compensating entries instead of UPDATEs, and nightly reconciliation jobs asserting ledger-sums-to-zero and no-duplicate-ownership, with alarms on violation.
- Choose plain managed PostgreSQL (PITR + synchronous replica) over CockroachDB/FoundationDB now; revisit only if you need multi-region active-active later — Nakama runs on either since it requires a Postgres-wire-compatible database.
- Settle the marketplace through a Brazilian marketplace-PSP with native Pix split (Mercado Pago split payments, Pagar.me, or Asaas ~0.99% Pix): buyer pays the PSP, funds release to the seller sub-account only after your economy core commits the item transfer, your fee is auto-split — this keeps you outside Banco Central payment-institution licensing (Res. BCB 506/2025 tightened requirements; May-2026 authorization window; R$15k caps on unauthorized players) and delegates seller KYC/KYB and MED fraud disputes to the PSP.
- Wire tournament prize pools as a segregated ledger account funded by marketplace fees; pay winners via the PSP's Pix payout API only after KYC (CPF + identity), withholding 30% IRRF on cash prizes (DARF 0916) per Receita Federal treatment of performance-based prizes — and retain Brazilian tax/gaming counsel before launch; do NOT charge player entry fees for prize tournaments initially, to stay clearly outside Lei 14.790/SPA betting regulation.
- Anti-cheat posture: keep 100% server-authoritative simulation (kills dupes/speed/teleport/damage injection by construction); the residual client-side surface is bots/input automation, hidden-state leakage, and timing manipulation — counter with interest management (never send fog-of-war state), per-action server-side type/range/rate validation, statistical input-pattern detection, replay recording for weekly-tournament review, shadow bans, and optional free Easy Anti-Cheat (via EOS) for PC builds; add dedicated anomaly detection on marketplace flows (wash trading, stolen-card cash-out via item sales).
- Ship weekly content drops as data, not client patches: versioned content bundles (item/creature/skill/biome definitions) published to CDN + server config, validated server-side, with feature flags and scheduled activation — either Heroic's Satori (paid) or a simple homegrown flags/config service; keep the authoritative stat tables server-side so clients can't spoof new content.
- Skip PlayFab (post-March-2026 Foundation Mode gates the free tier behind Xbox shipping; 1,000 lifetime accounts otherwise) and AccelByte (enterprise PCCU pricing) as the core; optionally use free EOS modules (Easy Anti-Cheat, voice, NAT relay) as complements — never for inventory/economy.
- If a mobile version is planned, keep the real-money marketplace and cash-out off iOS/Android clients (web/PC only, 'web shop' pattern): app-store policies around real-money trading and cash-out are restrictive and an account-ban risk; design the marketplace UI as a web surface from day one.

## Caveats
- Brazilian regulation is moving fast in 2026 (SPA/Lei 14.790 enforcement, Resolução BCB 506/2025, Pix MED 2.0, Receita Federal's new prize-tax tooling for bets/virtual competitions released March 2026): a real-money P2P marketplace with cash-out plus fee-funded prize pools sits close to several regulated perimeters (payment intermediation, betting, prize promotion) — everything here needs sign-off from Brazilian fintech/gaming counsel, not just engineering.
- Tax treatment of tournament prizes is fact-specific: performance-based prizes may be classed as remuneration (labor income) rather than 30%-exclusive-taxation prizes, changing withholding obligations; the 30% IRRF/DARF-0916 guidance found is from practitioner sources and must be confirmed for your exact structure.
- Several comparative sources (crux/gsb.supercraft.host) are published by a competing backend vendor and are biased toward self-hosted/alternative options; however the PlayFab Foundation Mode change is corroborated by Microsoft's own developer pages, and the 'Silent Sunset of PlayFab' sentiment appears independently on Medium.
- Nakama's open-source release cadence has slowed (v3.39.0 is the latest visible release, with sources disagreeing whether it shipped May 2025 or May 2026); Hiro and Satori are paid, closed components — deep reliance on them recreates the vendor-dependency you're avoiding, so keep the economy core independent of them.
- PSP terms can change: split-payment fees, settlement windows (D+1 vs D+30), and whether a PSP will accept a 'virtual game items' merchant category vary by provider and risk appetite — get written confirmation from Mercado Pago/Pagar.me/Asaas that game-item RMT with seller cash-out is an accepted use case before building against one.
- Search tooling was US-centric; some Brazilian specifics (BCB thresholds, IRRF mechanics) were verified only through secondary Portuguese-language sources rather than primary norms, and page summaries may compress details — re-verify the exact texts of Res. BCB 506/2025 and the RFB prize-tax rules.
- Self-hosting Nakama has meaningful hidden cost (DevOps/SRE, monitoring, upgrades, DB operations); if the team cannot staff this, Heroic Cloud DAU-based pricing changes the cost calculus and should be quoted directly.
- An RMT marketplace materially raises fraud stakes beyond anti-cheat: stolen-card laundering through item purchases, account-takeover-and-liquidate attacks, and chargeback abuse require rate limits, withdrawal delays/holds, device fingerprinting, and manual-review tooling that this research only outlines.
