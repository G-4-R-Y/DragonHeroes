# Research track: rl-creature-ai

## Summary
Tooling status (mid-2026): Unity ML-Agents is alive but slow-moving — the "deprecated" label in Unity 6000.1's Package Manager was officially confirmed an error (Unity, May 2025), and Release 23 / v4.0.0 shipped Sept 2, 2025 with Unity 6 minimum and the new Inference Engine 2.2.1 backend; treat it as maintained-but-not-strategic. Godot RL Agents remains a healthy community project (v0.8.2, Feb 2025; ~813 commits; backends: Stable-Baselines3, sample-factory, RLlib, CleanRL; experimental ONNX in-engine inference) but engine-in-the-loop throughput (~12k interactions/s) is 1-2 orders of magnitude too slow for serious self-play. The state of the art for indie budgets is PufferLib (2.0 paper at RLC 2025: 1M steps/s; 3.0 released 2025 with an improved PPO variant — Muon optimizer, GAE+VTrace advantage, prioritized trajectory replay — plus automatic hyperparameter tuning) which trains "tiny superhuman models in seconds" and runs full PPO at 300k–1.2M steps/s on a single RTX 4090 against its C-based envs, including a MOBA-style arena. Sample-factory remains a solid, maintained async-PPO alternative. The decisive architectural fact: fast training requires the combat sim to be a deterministic headless C/Rust library wrapped as a vectorized env — which Dragon Heroes needs anyway for server-authoritative combat, so training env and live server can share one codebase.

Compute and inference are non-issues for a 2D arena with small observation/action spaces. OpenAI Five-scale numbers (its from-scratch "Rerun" cost ~150 PetaFLOP/s-days over 2 months) reflect 5v5 Dota's enormous state space; a 1v1/3v3 2D arena at PufferLib throughputs yields competent policies in minutes-hours and strong self-play league policies in days on one consumer GPU — realistically R$5-30k in hardware or low hundreds of dollars of cloud per training iteration. Serving is nearly free: a <1M-parameter MLP/LSTM exported to ONNX Runtime infers in ~15-30 microseconds on CPU (INT8 quantization gives further 2-3x), so hundreds of concurrent bots at 10-20 Hz decision rates cost a fraction of one server core, and keeping weights server-side prevents the Rocket League "Nexto" problem (a community replay-pretrained bot so human-like that players used it to cheat in ranked). Shipped precedents now exist across scales: Gran Turismo Sophy went from free update (2.1 custom races) to paid content (Sophy 3.0 powering the Dec 2025 Power Pack DLC); Tencent's Juewu bots beat Honor of Kings pros and back deployed bot modes (open env: hok_env); Ubisoft La Forge shipped production RL work (For Honor defense bot, Roller Champions agents, GDC "Smart Bots for Better Games: RL in Production"); NCSoft's Blade & Soul arena agent hit 62% vs pros via self-play curriculum — the closest analog to a fighting-style ARPG duel; Apex Legends (June 2026) now fills low-skill lobbies with bots; and indie AI Arena (ArenaX Labs) shipped a whole game around player-trained imitation-learning fighters.

On the three specific questions: (a) The hybrid design is the industry-validated pattern — an Oct 2025 AMD Schola paper (Unreal) formalizes RL+behavior-tree NPCs, finding the hybrid far exceeds pure curriculum-RL success rates while approaching pure-BT reliability, and Ubisoft's production experience says the same: keep BT/utility AI for the hundreds of normal creatures (cheap to author, debuggable, designer-tunable — RL adds nothing there) and spend RL exclusively on elite bosses, PvP sparring bots, and BR lobby-filler where adaptiveness is the product. (b) Tournament-winner bots are a proven pipeline: behavioral-cloning pretraining from replays (VPT-style inverse dynamics models let you recover actions if you only logged states, but server-side obs+action logging from day one is far better), then RL fine-tuning with a KL penalty toward the BC policy to stay human-like, conditioned on the winner's build/loadout embedding. Caveats: one winner yields thin data (augment with population replays, condition on style), and you need explicit ToS consent to train on player replays — better, make it a feature ("fight last week's champion's ghost") with credit or marketplace revenue share. (c) Pitfalls are well-documented: reward hacking (CoastRunners infinite-checkpoint loop; OpenAI hide-and-seek agents exploiting physics — an infinite procedural world maximizes this attack surface, though that same property makes RL agents excellent pre-ship exploit finders, as EA did for Battlefield 2042/Dead Space QA); superhuman fairness (OpenAI Five's 217ms average reaction still read as "programmable-mouse telepathy"; AlphaStar's burst-APM controversy shows caps must bind on bursts, not averages — enforce reaction-time delay of 150-250ms, action-rate caps, aim noise, and identical observation interfaces inside the training loop, not post-hoc); and patch churn (OpenAI Five needed ~20 "surgery" operations over 296 days — from-scratch retrains would have turned 10 months into ~40). For weekly content drops, this last one is Dragon Heroes' biggest operational risk: it demands an entity-list/embedding observation schema where new items/skills/creatures are new embedding rows rather than new tensor shapes, warm-start fine-tuning each patch, and an automated evaluation gate before bots redeploy. No studio has publicly demonstrated weekly-cadence live bot retraining; assume you are building that pipeline yourselves.

## Recommendations
- Build the combat sim as a deterministic, headless, tick-based C/Rust library shared between the authoritative game server and a vectorized training env (PufferLib-style). This single decision makes RL cheap (100k+ steps/s), guarantees train/live parity, and you need the headless sim anyway for server-authoritative hitboxes.
- Adopt PufferLib 3.x (or sample-factory as fallback) for training; use Godot RL Agents / Unity ML-Agents only for early prototyping, not production training. PufferLib's default-hyperparameter PPO + auto-tuning is specifically designed for small teams without RL PhDs, and paid support exists (from ~$10k/mo) if you get stuck.
- Scope the hybrid exactly as proposed: behavior trees / utility AI for all normal creatures (designer-authored, predictable, patch-proof), RL policies only for elite bosses, PvP sparring/practice bots, and BR lobby fill in low-MMR brackets. This mirrors AMD Schola findings and Ubisoft production practice.
- Budget one RTX 4090/5090-class training box (or equivalent cloud) per concurrent experiment. For 1v1 arena policies with small obs/action spaces expect minutes-hours to competence and 1-5 days for a self-play league run (main agents + past checkpoints + exploiter agents, prioritized fictitious self-play to prevent strategy cycling).
- Instrument server-side replay logging (observation + action pairs, not video) from the first playtest. This is the cheapest thing you can do now and it unlocks behavioral cloning, tournament-champion ghosts, bot-vs-human evaluation, and cheat detection later.
- For champion bots: BC-pretrain on the winner's replays (plus population replays for coverage), RL fine-tune with a KL-divergence penalty toward the BC policy, and condition the policy on a build/skill-loadout embedding. Put replay-training consent in the tournament ToS and consider crediting/paying champions whose ghosts ship.
- Bake humanization into the training loop, not inference: enforced 150-250ms observation delay, hard caps on actions-per-second that bind on bursts (AlphaStar lesson), aim/targeting noise, and no privileged information beyond what a client at typical Brazilian latency (30-80ms to a São Paulo server) would see.
- Treat reward hacking as a certainty in an infinite procedural world: run adversarial evaluation, review top-return trajectories by hand each training run, penalize out-of-bounds/degenerate states, and repurpose unconstrained agents as automated exploit-finders on each weekly biome/content drop before it ships.
- Design the observation/action schema for weekly content drops on day one: entity-set encodings with learned embeddings for item/skill/creature IDs so new content is new rows, not new architecture; warm-start (surgery-style) fine-tunes each patch; automated eval gate (vs scripted suite + previous policies + win-rate/behavior regression checks) before bot redeploy.
- Serve policies as ONNX (INT8) on the game server CPU — at ~15-30 microseconds per decision, plan for hundreds of concurrent bots per core at 10-20 Hz; never ship policy weights to clients, since a leaked strong policy becomes a ranked-cheating and marketplace-farming tool (Nexto precedent, amplified by your real-money Pix marketplace).

## Caveats
- No studio has publicly demonstrated weekly-cadence retraining of live RL bots; the OpenAI Five 'surgery' evidence is from a 10-month research run, so the weekly-patch pipeline is genuinely novel engineering risk for Dragon Heroes.
- PufferLib's 1M steps/s figures are measured on its own optimized C environments; your throughput depends entirely on how fast your own headless sim runs — an engine-in-the-loop Godot sim would be ~100x slower and change the compute math.
- Key ecosystem projects are small-team or single-maintainer efforts (PufferLib/Puffer.ai, Godot RL Agents, sample-factory); Unity ML-Agents, though officially not deprecated (May 2025 statement), has had roughly one release per year — factor in abandonment risk and avoid deep coupling.
- Pro-beating results (Blade & Soul, Juewu, GT Sophy, AlphaStar) all had corporate-scale compute and dedicated research teams; the claim that a 2D small-obs arena is orders of magnitude cheaper is well-founded but not proven for your exact game until you benchmark your own sim.
- Human-likeness is hard to get fully right: even with 217ms reaction delays OpenAI Five read as inhuman due to coordination and precision; expect iteration on humanization and player perception testing, especially for sparring bots meant to feel like practice partners.
- Tournament-winner replay data will be thin (one player, limited hours); BC on it alone overfits, and legal/consent handling for training on player data (including LGPD considerations in Brazil) was not deeply researched here — validate with counsel.
- Apex Legends' 2026 matchmaking bots' training method is unspecified (may be scripted, not RL), so cite it as evidence of demand for player-like bots, not of shipped RL.
- Search results were US-centric (tool limitation); there may be relevant Brazilian/LatAm academic or studio work not captured, and GDC 2026 material was only partially indexed at research time.
